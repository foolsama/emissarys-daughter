# Alonythil

Instead of asking:

> “Is the mentor a villain or not?”
> 

Ask:

> “Is the mentor right?”
> 

The mentor should represent:

- institutional correctness,
- methodological rigor,
- emotional detachment,
- and a worldview that *works* — until it doesn’t.

That makes him dangerous *later*, without needing him to twirl a mustache now.
He’s not Darth Vader.

He’s the man who files Vader’s reports.

In other words, he is **what Alenawe thought the system was**.

He’s not her father figure.

He’s not her conscience.

He’s not her rival.

He is the proof that the system *can* function — just not under the conditions the Dragonborn creates.

Give him one line that reveals he knows what he's surrendered:
Alonythil: "I was like you, once. I asked the same questions."
Alenawe: "What changed?"
Alonythil: "They didn't lead in a useful direction."
This makes his path tempting, not just sad.

### Alonythil's trajectory:
**Game start:** Personal handler. Falkreath. Watching her watch the locals. He writes reports; she learns the craft. He knows the destabilization posture. She doesn't. His job is to make her useful within the system — not to make her understand the system.
**Helgen redirect:** The directive comes to him, not her. He absorbs the scope expansion and tasks her with tracking TLD. She thinks her world is expanding from governance to intelligence. His world is expanding from one promising junior to managing the operation that creates chaos for the Dominion's benefit while his best student stabilizes everything she touches.
**Companion phase:** She files independently. Her reports are accurate, clean, institutionally legible. She describes TLD resolving crises, stabilizing regions, building alliances. She describes effective governance by someone who isn't governing. From her perspective: valuable intelligence. From his: a detailed account of his best operative doing counter-mission work and getting better at it every week.
Meanwhile he's being promoted. The quality of intelligence flowing from her reports (which he forwards) is part of why. He's rising on the product of someone working against the objective he's rising to coordinate. That's the irony he lives inside.

### By MinQ2: 
Regional intelligence coordinator for Skyrim. He's managing the destabilization posture. He reads her reports and sees: she's been effective. At the wrong thing. The institution's model says "promote and extract" based on her excellent reporting. His private calculation says "extract before someone above me notices the actual field activity doesn't match the strategic posture."

He arrives at the debrief managing a circuit — she's one stop among many, the stop he lingers on longest, and neither of them will acknowledge that. His authority is no longer personal. It's positional. The warmth that remains is vestigial — habit, not intention. When it surfaces, it surprises both of them.

**The constraint that defines him in this scene:** Everything he says has to survive being overheard by TLD — a kill-on-sight designee standing three feet away. He cannot name the destabilization posture. He cannot explain why her effectiveness is the problem. He cannot tell her the real reason the extraction is urgent. Every deflection, every institutional non-answer, every redirect — he's not just managing her argument. He's managing the room.

**How he resolves, per path:**
- Path A (field reclassification): He files paperwork. There's a form for this. Bureaucracy as temporal mercy.
- Path B (outgrown the framework): He defers. There is no form. "That is noted" — institutional retreat from what he can't categorize.
- Path C (network proposal): He flags it for strategic review. The most dangerous outcome — if approved, the Dominion funds its own counter-mission infrastructure. If rejected, she'll ask why, and the "why" is classified.
- Path 2X (player indifferent): The only path where he can argue back — the player handed him a clean, unclassified weapon. The debate ends in mutual constraint: she can't name the personal reason, he can't name the strategic one. Same evidence, opposite conclusions. His coded closing — "the operational assessment has merit... that is what I can do with what you have given me" — mirrors her silence. Both sentences mean: there is more, and I cannot say it here.

**The fracture reveal (universal):** He held the Alinor desk she's being offered. Three years. He found the answers — the destabilization posture, the strategic logic, the full machinery. Understanding it didn't let him change it, stop it, or object in terms the system would process. He went back to the field. He is now offering her the exact position he fled. It didn't work for him, either, but the system says this is correct. 

**Emotional baseline:** "I have already accepted what you are still fighting. I would like you to fight it better than I did. I cannot say that."
