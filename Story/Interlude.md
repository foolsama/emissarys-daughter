# Interlude - Embassadorial Fallout

Elenwen is not Alenawe's handler. Alenawe reports through Alonythil → up the chain. Elenwen receives filtered excerpts at best. The assignment to Skyrim is a dual test: can Alenawe perform independently, and can Elenwen resist the urge to interfere with her daughter's career?
That means the courier letter isn't an operational directive from a superior. It's a mother breaking protocol to contact her daughter after a crisis — while trying to sound like it's operational.
The letter doesn't carry institutional authority. It carries emotional authority wearing institutional clothing.

Interlude will carry three major revelations:
1. She know about the infiltration and chose not to warn the Embassy
2. She now has to decide what version of events to report
3. Elenwen's letter makes that professional decision personally consequential

### The Pre-DI Problem: She Knew
This is the real twist. As a follower, Alenawe was present for the planning. She heard Delphine's scheme. She knows the player is going to infiltrate her mother's embassy.
She doesn't warn the Embassy. Why?
Not loyalty to the player (not yet, not at this affinity level). Positioning.
1.	The intelligence the player needs is real and important — TLD needs the Blades' trust and the Thalmor dossiers to progress on the dragon threat
2.	Warning the Embassy burns her cover with the Dragonborn and the Blades — she becomes useless to everyone
3.	The Thalmor dossiers the player will find contain information she herself might want access to — she may not know what's in Elenwen's private files
4.	She may calculate that the Embassy's security should be able to handle one person at a party — she underestimates TLD (again)
5.	Intervening would mean choosing a side. She's not ready to choose.
So she lets it happen. Not out of betrayal. Out of strategic non-intervention. She stays in the position where she has the most options.

### What does she report?
Her dead drop report covering the DI period is the first time she actively edits reality. Before this, her reports were probably honest — clinical, but accurate. 

Now:

What she knows:
•	The player planned to infiltrate the Embassy
•	The player succeeded beyond any reasonable prediction
•	Multiple Thalmor are dead
•	Intelligence was taken
•	A prisoner was freed
•	A mole was burned

What she files:
She can't omit DI entirely — other sources will report it. Her silence on an event this large would be a red flag. So she has to write something. The question is what she leaves out:
What she includes															What she omits
Subject was involved in the Embassy incident (other sources will confirm)	That she knew about the plan in advance
Subject demonstrated capabilities exceeding prior assessment				Specific details of how the infiltration was planned
Recommend updated threat classification										That the Blades were involved (protects TLD's alliance network)
Subject's focused on the dragon threat, not anti-Thalmor ideology			Anything the player told her in confidence about what they found

She writes the column-two version. The legible simplification. Just enough truth to be credible, just enough omission to be useful to her position.

And then it goes *catastrophically worse than anyone predicted.*

## The Letter

Elenwen is not issuing orders. She doesn't have that authority over Alenawe. She's doing something worse: she's a mother reaching for her daughter through the only language they share.

	*"I trust you are well.
	You will have heard about the incident. I am unharmed. The situation is being managed. 
	I have been told your current assignment places you near the individual involved. I was not consulted on this placement. I want you to know that.
	I am confident in your judgment. I am less confident in your positioning. Consider whether your current deployment serves your long-term trajectory, or merely your current one.
	You do not need to respond to this.
	— E."*
	
	Alenawe,
	I trust you are well.
	You will have heard about the incident; I am unharmed. Several of my staff are not. I will not discuss details through this channel.
	I have since learned that your current assignment places you in proximity to the individual responsible. I was not consulted on this placement, and I will be addressing that.
	You are talented. You are not positioned for what this has become. This is no longer observation. It is no longer research. The person you are traveling with walked into a secure facility, killed people I am responsible for, and walked out. Whatever you think you are studying, that is what you are standing next to.
	I am asking you — not ordering you, I no longer have that — to consider whether you are where you should be.
	Do not be brave. Be correct.
	— Mother

## What the Player Sees
She receives the courier. She reads it. She doesn't share it. She has work to do - she has to write this report.

Her next interactions are subtly different — not dramatically. The player who's paying attention notices:
•	She's quieter
•	She asks one question she wouldn't have asked before — something about the player's plans, or allies, or next move
•	She might say something like: "I've received... updated parameters. My reporting obligations have changed. I want you to be aware of that, to the extent I'm able."
That line is her being as honest as she can: I am now required to report on you more aggressively, and I'm telling you this because I respect you enough not to do it silently.
The player who hears that and stays is demonstrating trust. The player who hears it and doesn't react is demonstrating either trust or indifference — and she can't tell which. Both are tracked.
