# Major Mission 1

1. Khajit trader asks Dragonborn for help.
2. Alenawe becomes interested.
3. Mine reveals a governance problem.
4. Dragonborn fixes it.
5. Alenawe spends the aftermath trying to explain why that worked.
6. Realizes her explanation isn't sufficient.

## Purpose / Narrative Goal

MinQ1 starts with: Skyrim is crude but functional.
MajQ1 should make her ask: Functional for what?

Possible pressure:
- does ordeal actually identify good judgment, or just audacity?
- does success under pressure produce legitimacy, or merely attention?
- does quick status revision reveal truth, or reward volatility?

MajQ1 should not be:
- "find a politically interesting place in Skyrim"
It should be:
- "investigate a pattern of instability whose physical expression can live in a random hostile site."

And incentives are exactly where institutional critique becomes interesting.

**A broken mine is boring.**

A mine that remains broken because:
- the steward benefits,
- the local merchant benefits,
- a noble benefits,
- a guild benefits,
- or even the Jarl benefits,
is much more interesting.

Now Alenawe's realization isn't "The institution failed." It's"The institution may be succeeding."
Just not for the people it claims to serve.

Is this failed governance, private governance, or governance hiding behind the appearance of failure?
- assess whether disruption to this extraction corridor is incidental, criminal, or strategically patterned
- determine whether local authority remains functionally competent
- recommend stabilization, observation, or quiet nonintervention

The mission should end with **recommendation pressure**, not just combat completion.

### The Player Is Not the Camera - The Player Is the Proof

## Before Site

Alenawe has a clean model:
- a mine matters because it feeds transport and allocation
- disorder here has regional consequences
- Alenawe builds models about possible causes.

"The Jarl says bandits.
The guards say bandits.
I've traded this route twenty years.
These aren't bandits."

Alenawe immediately starts asking:

How long?
Which shipments?
Which routes?
Why hasn't the Jarl responded?
Who profits from the delay?

She is smart here. Her analysis should be visibly competent. The player should see her framework working well - because what matters is watching it stop being sufficient.

## At Site

Evidence reveals the pattern is not random:
- certain shipments were untouched
- certain workers were removed, not killed
- certain buyers or routes recur
- "hostiles" are too well supplied, too selective, or too informed

Now the player acts. And this is where MajQ1 diverges from an intelligence exercise.

The player resolves the site in whatever way they resolve sites. The point is that Alenawe is watching, and what she sees proves something her framework does not like:
- they can restore function without sanction
- they can produce loyalty without office
- they can reduce suffering without preserving the official arrangement
- or they can bypass the whole chain and still be obeyed

**Design constraint:** The site must be structured so that multiple play styles each produce a legible "proof" moment. A stealth player who ghosts the entire mine and leaves evidence for her to find is as valid as a combat player who kills everyone and then helps survivors. What matters is that the outcome is achieved personally, quickly and efficiently, and she witnesses it.

Behavioral tracking during the site should feed Stability/Affinity:
- restraint under opportunity: +Stability
- efficiency without collateral: +Stability
- excessive force / chaos: -Stability
- freeing captives / aiding workers: +Affinity (small)
These do not gate content. They weight the aftermath conversation.

## After Site

The key issue is not "the enemies are dead."
It is:
- Why did local authority fail to solve this?

The mine is where Alenawe discovers that institutions do not necessarily protect value; they protect value that remains legible, administrable, and politically useful.

So her questions become:
- who or what exactly is the system for?
- Why was the Dragonborn needed?
And says something like:

"That's not actually the interesting question."

Player:

"No?"

Alenawe:

"No."

"The interesting question is why none of the people already responsible for this chose to solve it."

### How late recruitment changes this

Requires a different reason she brings the player:

#### Pre-DI
- standard observational assignment
- player is useful access and protection
- tone is professional curiosity
- she expects confirmation of her model
- the player is a research variable

#### Post-DI
- the institution's model is already under strain
- she wants a live comparison - not casually, urgently
- the player is not just escort; they are a known destabilizing variable
- she is testing whether her own reclassification of TLD (post-Embassy breach) holds under operational conditions
- the mine becomes a controlled experiment she is running on her own beliefs


## The Setup

A Khajit trader requests assistance with a disruption affecting a mine, 3+ days after MinQ1.

The player receives the quest via courier letter (like Falkreath Jarl mission).

Alenawe expresses unusual interest in the situation and asks to accompany the investigation.

The mission: A mine with disrupted shipments
- reports say "bandit takeover"
- records show selective predation and off-book cooperation
- the site proves the instability is structured, not chaotic
- the player resolves it by force, stealth, or selective interference
- Alenawe then gets to infer why this was not resolved
The conversation becomes:

"Why was this allowed to continue?"
"Why did nobody fix it?"
"Why did you fix it when nobody else did?"

That keeps her in "scholar of governance" territory.

A selected mine contains evidence that reclassifies the situation:
Bandits are unusually organized.
Leader has a ledger.
Ledger shows they know shipment schedules.

Alenawe's question becomes:
"How did they know that?"

Alenawe's task:
- classify the disruption
- determine whether it represents simple disorder or useful leverage
- identify who benefits from the current arrangement
- **Understanding why the Dragonborn solved it *now* becomes the real mission.**

Fixed pieces:
- Letter from a "Concerned merchant", delivered by courier, to start the quest.
- the suspicious note/ledger on the leader
- a defined question (classify the disruption)
- an opinion/interpretation structure
- an aftermath conversation with Alenawe - this is the real climax of MajQ1

Variable piece:
- the actual hostile site selected from criteria
